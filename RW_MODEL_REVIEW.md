# SAT Sprint Reading and Writing review

Reviewed November 5, 2026. Website: [SAT Sprint](https://rk68193-sys.github.io/SAT-Method/). Repository: [SAT-Method](https://github.com/rk68193-sys/SAT-Method), starting commit `71e331f920fc23d48a8884bb1f1d124e6f19ca26`.

The site has substantial coverage already. Its main weakness was inconsistency between careful explanations in the deeper lessons and broader instructions at the entrance to the site. This pass aligns those instructions and the general practice flow with the combined Reading and Writing model developed from the supplied Meltzer guides.

Implementation branch: `codex/rw-model-improvements`. The generated `index.html` is rebuilt. GitHub Pages publishes the page from `main`; publication status is available in the repository's [Actions history](https://github.com/rk68193-sys/SAT-Method/actions).

## What already matches the model

- All four official domains have coverage: Information and Ideas, Craft and Structure, Expression of Ideas, and Standard English Conventions. Vocabulary, function, cross-text connections, notes and graphs are present; the site does not need those areas added from scratch.
- The deeper reading lessons already distinguish function from content, hypothetical supporting evidence from passage facts, claim strength, attribution, and the need for factual accuracy in synthesis.
- Grammar instruction includes clause structure, verb agreement, tense, verb form, pronouns, possession and modifiers. Worked explanations and question IDs provide useful connections to the source bank.
- The advertised section timing is already correct: 54 questions in 64 minutes, divided into two 32-minute modules. About 71 seconds per question is an average, not an individual deadline. [College Board test structure](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/sat/whats-on-the-test/structure)
- The site identifies official question provenance and separates its authored teaching from College Board material.

## Discrepancies and changes made

| Finding in the starting version | Why it matters | Change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The reading sidebar said “adapted to the job,” but the routine heading required the same four steps, in order, on every question. It also required tagging every sentence and predicting before viewing choices. | A brief detail question, a dense inference, a vocabulary item and a notes goal do not require identical processing. Mandatory annotation can add work without improving understanding. | Rewrote the routine, tag lesson, prediction lesson, guided-example framing, clock prompts and entrance copy. Tags and predictions are optional scaffolds; evidence may span several sentences. |
| General Practice offered standard, fast and relaxed countdowns, with no untimed option for the full bank. | Students need to establish comprehension and conventions before speed becomes an objective. A slower learner should not receive a speed failure while learning a rule. | Added **Learn · untimed**, the default when no clock preference is saved. It creates no countdown, elapsed-time record, speed graph or under/over rating. Existing clock preferences remain valid. |
| The notes drill displayed only the goal and choices before answering; notes appeared in the explanation afterward. | Goal fit does not establish factual accuracy. This directly conflicted with the site's stronger synthesis guidance. | All 204 notes drill items now expose their notes before answering. The prompt requires both goal fit and accurate use of the notes. Its practice target allows more time for that fuller task. |
| The short prediction guidance reduced support/weaken to a few causal patterns and told students to look for where Text 2 pushes back. | Supporting claims can be descriptive or comparative. Weakening can expose an alternative explanation or a faulty link. Paired texts can agree or extend a view. | Expanded the task-specific expectations and replaced the assumption of cross-text opposition with an attributed comparison of the requested issue. Expanded vocabulary guidance beyond the blank's sentence when other context matters. |
| A punctuation summary prescribed comma-before/strong-mark-after for “however” or “though.” Another shortcut checked only whether the material after a mark could stand alone. | Transition placement depends on which statement it modifies. A semicolon needs independent clauses on both sides, while a list semicolon has another role. | Replaced the summary with clause and meaning checks and examples of transitions opening and closing clauses. Qualified the list-semicolon note and the rule about matching dashes around an actual aside. |
| The possessive shortcut asked whether a noun owns the next noun and crossed out apostrophes before verbs or prepositions. The aside test relied on removability alone. | Noun adjacency does not prove possession; a possessive can stand alone. Grammatical removability alone does not determine whether identifying information is essential. | Added construction, number and meaning checks, noun-adjunct and standalone-possessive examples, contraction expansion, and an identifying-reference test for essential information. |
| The main grammar method promoted rarely correct forms as a way to break ties. Frequency cards carried labels such as “Rule it out” and “Points to the answer.” | Counts from one bank are descriptive. They cannot justify rejecting a grammatical progressive verb, passive construction, contraction or transition on an unfamiliar item. | Replaced the tie-breaking instruction with a structural/meaning comparison. Reframed the frequency page and labels as observations from the exported bank. The underlying historical counts are retained, not presented as a validated prediction model. |
| Set summaries combined first attempts and repeats; saved results mainly tracked correctness and time. The same ID could appear untried when reached through another view. | Familiar answers can inflate apparent progress. A correct guess needs review, and the cause of an error is more useful than a wrong/right total alone. | Added first-recorded versus repeat breakdowns, shared history by question ID, optional confidence before answering, review of unsure/guessed correct answers, root-cause selection and an evidence/rule note. Saved reviews survive repeat attempts and reloads and are accessible from setup. |
| The footer called the content College Board's “full question bank.” | A fixed export is not evidence of completeness relative to a changing live bank. | Changed the wording to identify an export. |

The first-recorded label is deliberately limited: it does not prove that a learner has never seen a question in a lesson, another app or an official test. Practice sets also retain immediate feedback after each answer. They are learning tools, not an adaptive SAT simulation. The setup now links to [official Bluebook practice](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/practice/practice-tests/bluebook) for full-test rehearsal.

## Further improvements worth prioritizing

1. **Accessible question text and data.** Restored image cards often expose an image description containing only the question ID and answer buttons labeled A–D. Preserve the source images, but add reviewed text, actual option labels and accessible tables or graph descriptions. This needs content verification, particularly for the 131 restored figure cards and 335 additional grammar cards; a generic alt description cannot replace their content.
2. **Practice by official domain and skill.** Current filters are mostly question source, grammar part, difficulty and attempt status. Map the local jobs to official skills so a student can select mixed questions within a weak skill, then review fresh accuracy and uncertainty. Keep small-set results separate from SAT score predictions. [Official Reading and Writing overview](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/sat/whats-on-the-test/reading-writing)
3. **Reduce the initial page payload.** The rebuilt page is about 5.94 MB before transfer compression, including both subjects' data. Split question data by subject or skill and load it when needed, with a versioned cache. Measure cold-load performance before and after; this review did not run a network performance benchmark.
4. **Version the source inventory.** Record the export date, source hashes, item counts and review status in a visible content manifest. Compare future official exports by ID and rerun source and answer checks when importing changes.

## Validation and limits

- `node src/check/rw-practice.js`: passes. It checks 1,845 unique R&W IDs, cross-view attempt history, untimed/paced distinctions, legacy records, uncertainty review, preservation of saved review notes, and pre-answer access to the notes in all 204 drill items.
- Preservation comparison against the starting Git commit: official passages, choices, answer keys, IDs, difficulty records, question memberships and source annotations are unchanged. Authored lesson/routine instructions changed; no book questions or scanned book pages were added to the site.
- `python -I src/check/study_coverage.py`: passes, preserving 249 math types, 1,925 memberships and 998 scoped checks.
- Assembly and JavaScript syntax checks pass. Theme token contrast checks and `git diff --check` pass.
- Chrome UI checks covered untimed grammar, text/worked reading, notes and source-image practice; paced answers; pause/resume across navigation; keyboard answers after confidence selection; Enter inside the review textarea; uncertain correct answers; partial-set results; repeats; saved notes after reload; revised grammar/reading pages; and shared Math navigation. No console errors were observed in that session.
- Narrow-screen checks used a 390-pixel viewport and found no horizontal document overflow in the new question and review flow. The confidence controls, select and textarea remained usable. The viewport override was reset afterward.

This is a targeted instructional and behavior review. It is not a fresh solution-by-solution verification of all 1,845 questions against the original College Board PDFs, a screen-reader accessibility audit, or evidence that a particular routine raises scores. Existing counts and annotations were preserved rather than independently remeasured.
